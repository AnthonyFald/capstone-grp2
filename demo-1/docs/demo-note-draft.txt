Demo 1 — Assisted Meeting Documentation

This demo tests whether free local AI can help capture construction-meeting decisions, responsibilities, deadlines, and unresolved questions. It supports the thesis that better documentation could reduce avoidable RFIs, but it does not directly measure RFI reduction.

A local companion processes recorded English audio with Whisper and speaker-detection models. It groups voices across the recording, aligns words with speaker turns, and suggests names and roles from explicit introductions. Users correct the transcript and speaker labels. The website then runs Qwen locally to read the discussion in context and draft concise notes linked to supporting source turns. The model analyzes future commitments, decisions/restrictions, and unanswered questions in the full discussion before producing structured notes. Owners and deadlines are checked against cited evidence. No paid API or AI account powers the app.

Two fictional repair-coordination text scenarios and a synthetic single-speaker recording are included, along with generation scripts. They are not actual Evergreen records. Starter reference checklists were authored with Codex assistance and need independent team review.

Development tests verified real local transcription, speaker separation on an official two-speaker sample, name/role suggestions on synthetic audio, transcript editing/import/export, and browser AI inference. Earlier line-by-line classification lost context and included irrelevant fragments. The revised pipeline uses discussion context, explicit extraction criteria, structured output, and analysis of the final status of actions, decisions and unresolved questions. Summarization and transcription errors remain possible. Development checks establish a functional workflow, not thesis-level accuracy or time savings. Independent evaluation on real team meetings is still needed.

A successful experiment would provide preliminary evidence about documentation quality, not proof of a 30% reduction in real RFIs. Small synthetic samples and required human review limit generalization.
