Demo 1 — Assisted Meeting Documentation

This demo tests whether free local AI can help capture construction-meeting decisions, responsibilities, deadlines, and unresolved questions. It supports the thesis that better documentation could reduce avoidable RFIs, but it does not directly measure RFI reduction.

A local companion processes recorded English audio with Whisper and speaker-detection models. It groups voices across the recording, aligns words with speaker turns, and suggests names and roles from explicit introductions. Users correct the transcript and speaker labels. The website then runs Qwen locally to classify relevant transcript lines. Notes preserve source wording; simple patterns fill explicit owners and deadlines. No paid API or AI account powers the app.

Two fictional repair-coordination text scenarios and a synthetic single-speaker recording are included, along with generation scripts. They are not actual Evergreen records. Starter reference checklists were authored with Codex assistance and need independent team review.

Development tests verified real local transcription, speaker separation on an official two-speaker sample, name/role suggestions on synthetic audio, transcript editing/import/export, and browser AI inference. Earlier free-form note generation invented or confused details; the final design instead classifies source lines and preserves their wording. Classification and transcription errors remain possible. These checks establish a functional workflow, not thesis-level accuracy or time savings. Mac execution and real team meetings still need testing.

Compare against the proposed 25% reduction in preparation time and 20% reduction in missed details; do not treat source-line matches as accuracy.

Users can inspect sources, correct notes, compare the original output with references, and export results. A rules-only baseline is clearly labeled. A successful experiment would provide preliminary evidence about documentation quality, not proof of a 30% reduction in real RFIs. Small synthetic samples and required human review limit generalization.
