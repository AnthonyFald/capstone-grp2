REVISED THESIS · MID-QUARTER

We hypothesize that AI-assisted retrieval of authorized documents, followed by human verification, will reduce avoidable RFI submissions and total review time without incorrectly closing questions that need formal clarification.

The original 30% target lacked a measured baseline. Neither completed demo establishes a reduction in real RFIs. See what changed and why →

When information exists,
but doesn’t reach the crew.

Decisions, deadlines, and responsibilities are scattered across meeting notes, daily reports, and schedules. Subcontractors may repeat questions, miss updates, or plan work using outdated information.

A Request for Information (RFI) formally asks for clarification. Our focus is on avoidable requests whose answers already exist in the project record. Necessary RFIs remain part of the process.

03 / OUR APPROACH

Connect the documents. Keep people in control.

We take the subcontractor’s perspective: what has changed, who is responsible, and what needs clarification before work can proceed?

  1. 01

    Collect

    Bring together authorized specifications, drawings, existing meeting minutes, daily reports, schedules, and relevant change orders.

  2. 02

    Connect

    Use AI to extract decisions, link source information, and flag missing or conflicting details.

  3. 03

    Review & act

    People verify findings, approve updates, and submit a formal RFI when clarification is still needed.

Surface delays, inconsistencies, and unresolved issues.

Scenario & test

Flooring crews submit daily reports on completed repairs, complications, and outstanding work. An AI agent identifies delays, conflicting updates, and issues other contractors need to know about.

Comparison

Compare findings with a predefined list of issues deliberately included in the synthetic reports.

03

RFI triage

Find existing answers and escalate genuine unknowns.

Scenario & test

Subcontractors ask about materials, responsibilities, repair procedures, and deadlines. The AI searches current, authorized records, checks document revisions, then returns a source to verify or flags the need for a formal RFI.

Comparison

Label questions before testing. Compare avoidable submissions, necessary escalation, and incorrect closures with and without AI assistance; include outdated documents in the test.

Success measures

Unnecessary RFIs avoided · Correctly answered questions (%) · Questions incorrectly marked as resolved

04

Schedule & task coordination

Update work assignments without missing dependencies.

Scenario & test

Removing and replacing damaged flooring introduces new tasks and dependencies. The AI combines meeting notes, reports, and schedules to suggest priorities, assignments, and revised deadlines.

Comparison

Assess AI-assisted proposals against manually prepared schedules for conflicts and overlooked responsibilities.

Success measures

Scheduling conflicts identified · Missed task dependencies · Schedule preparation time

05 / DATA & EVALUATION

A real case. A controlled test environment.

Public records establish the context

Construction updates, bidding information, and change orders ground the project in Evergreen’s documented circumstances. They do not provide the internal records needed to reconstruct actual subcontractor coordination.

Synthetic records make testing possible

Fictional meeting transcripts and daily reports now support the first two demos. The daily-report pilot includes 10 supplied records, a six-report transfer scenario and a three-report negative control. Known gaps, later resolutions and conflicting accounts provide reference issues for evaluation. Schedules and question-answering remain planned.

How we’ll judge usefulness

  • Accuracy: Are extracted facts and answers supported by the records?
  • Completeness: What important issues, actions, or dependencies were missed?
  • Efficiency: How much time and repetitive communication can be avoided?
  • Errors: Did the AI incorrectly resolve a question or suggest a conflicting schedule?
Demo 2 evaluation

Our provisional goals are at least 25% less total review time and 20% fewer missed issues, with no increase in false alarms and final accuracy that matches or exceeds manual review. Human-only and AI-assisted human reviews must be measured separately from model processing speed. Source-linked evidence, negative controls, and preserved original outputs make mistakes reviewable. Synthetic pilot results do not establish performance on live projects.

06 / MID-QUARTER ANALYSIS

What we learned. What we changed.

The completed analysis brings together our two demos, Trent McMorrow’s stakeholder interview, class feedback, and a revised testing plan. Our original RFI-reduction thesis is not yet validated.

Promising results, clearer limits

Demo 1 cut preparation time by 54.1% before verification, but average accuracy was lower than manual notes. Demo 2 covered all 12 expected issue categories in the ten-report comparison; its timing claim remains unverified.

Sources, permission, and human judgment

The interview shifted our focus toward authorized documents, visible source revisions, and a clear distinction between discussion and formal direction. Our next tests will count checking time and preserve questions that need an RFI.
