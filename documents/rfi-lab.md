# RFI Lab · concrete flooring removal

**Team 2 classroom scenario.** The team used Evergreen High School's documented water-damage repair and Change Order No. 1 to practice drafting a request for information. SUB-RFI-001 is a simulated subcontractor request. The classroom dates below belong to the exercise.

| Field | Team draft |
| --- | --- |
| Request | SUB-RFI-001 · Clarification of concrete flooring removal scope |
| Sender and recipient | Concrete flooring subcontractor to general contractor / construction manager |
| Issued / response requested | October 13 / October 20, 2026 |
| References | Change Order No. 1, applicable architectural and structural drawings, concrete flooring specifications |
| Priority | High because the removal limits and release sequence affect cost and schedule |

## What the subcontractor needs to know

The change order described concrete floor removal following water damage. The subcontractor needed the limits of demolition, the condition of the remaining slab, and the requirements for restoring the area before assigning labor, ordering material, or coordinating the next trade. The team draft asked the GC to obtain the following information from the architect and design team.

1. **Removal limits.** Identify affected areas and dimensions. Does removal extend past visibly damaged concrete?
2. **Slab condition.** Identify the inspection or testing needed to determine whether the existing slab and substrate are suitable for the next work.
3. **Replacement specifications.** Confirm thickness, mix, reinforcement, surface preparation, curing, and finish.
4. **Moisture testing.** Identify the test and acceptance criteria required before flooring or moisture-sensitive finishes can be installed.
5. **Scope and compensation.** Clarify covered demolition, disposal, preparation, testing, and replacement activities. Identify how to obtain written authorization for additional work.
6. **Sequence and approval.** Confirm the order of removal, inspection, testing, replacement, and flooring installation, including required holds and releases.

## Connection to the experiments

A reviewer could start by checking an existing approved drawing or specification for each question. A missing boundary, conflicting direction, or unsigned release still calls for clarification. That distinction became the basis for the later RFI experiments. The lab produced a written request. It had no model, prompt, runtime, scoring baseline, or recorded project response.
